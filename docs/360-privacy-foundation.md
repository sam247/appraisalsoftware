# Anonymous 360 privacy foundation — 18 September 2026

This is a database foundation, not a released 360 workflow. Customer-facing 360 remains disabled. Annual appraisals remain identified. Stripe and marketing are unchanged.

## Threat model and anonymity contract (written before schema changes)

Normal organisation administrators, managers, subjects, other members, reviewers, cross-tenant users and browser/API clients must never receive anonymous feedback-to-reviewer linkage. Tenant administrators may see invitation identities and completion states, but not individual feedback records, record IDs, submission timestamps or ordered response streams. Subject/manager status alone grants no report access; the initial report endpoint requires organisation administrator authority.

Trusted database/infrastructure operators and service-role code can access the identity mapping and are outside the anonymity guarantee. Bearer invitation possession authorises access to that reviewer's own draft in the future respondent implementation. The system cannot protect a reviewer who shares their link, identifies themselves in text, is recognised by writing style, or participates in a cohort where others disclose their answers. It cannot guarantee statistical anonymity against collusion, crafted questions or external knowledge.

V1 reporting rule: anonymous external reviewers form one combined cohort, never relationship subgroups. Require at least **five submitted reviewers**, and at least five non-empty answers for each reported question. Release results only after campaign closure. Five is an explicitly chosen conservative product floor against trivial one/two-person identification, not a mathematical anonymity guarantee. No self responses in the anonymous cohort. Separate identified self comparison and identified 360 are deferred.

Free-text wording for the eventual respondent screen: “Your organisation receives feedback without reviewer names or response times. Feedback is combined with at least four other reviewers and released after the campaign closes. Avoid personal references that identify you; your writing itself may reveal your identity. Trusted platform operators can access operational records.” This wording must not be published as an available capability until the complete workflow passes integration tests.

## Existing access audit

`public.responses.assignment_id` joins `public.campaign_assignments.respondent_person_id` to People. Org-admin SELECT policies expose responses and answers. Existing respondent resolve creates a public response, and annual result queries explicitly join assignments. These paths must never collect anonymous 360 payloads. The existing outbox is service-only after the previous release, but contains bearer links; it stays in the identity/delivery plane. Existing reminder and close functions operate on assignments/tokens and should retain those responsibilities. There are no application exports to extend. Disclosurely code was not supplied in this working context; no unrelated repository was searched.

## Architecture to implement

- Private campaign privacy contract: one subject, fixed anonymous mode and five-response floor, independent of mutable public settings JSON.
- Private feedback records and answers: campaign/organisation/question only, no reviewer identity, relationship or timestamps. Separate private mapping connects assignment to feedback for future save/resume and exactly-once submission.
- No ordinary role has table access; RLS has no ordinary-role policies. Only trusted service operations/database-definer endpoints can access records.
- Closed-only administrator report RPC returns question IDs/prompts and safe aggregates. No feedback/assignment/reviewer IDs, raw individual ratings, per-response chronology or relationship categories. Text is sorted lexically, independently per question, rather than by reviewer or submission order.
- Database launch guards block non-annual campaigns from the identified pipeline and block anonymous settings on annual campaigns. These guards stay until the complete 360 engine replaces those paths safely.

The mapping and feedback storage retain a trusted-operator join. This intentionally enforces anonymity against tenant/application roles, not cryptographic anonymity against the platform.

## Implementation and delivery report (A–O)

**A. Functionality:** private privacy contracts, feedback storage, an operator-only invitation/feedback mapping, rating/text validation, immutable submitted state and an administrator aggregate-report RPC are implemented. Launch guards prevent a non-annual campaign or anonymous annual configuration from collecting through the existing identified pipeline. No customer-facing 360 creation, sending or respondent workflow is enabled.

**B–C. Guarantee/threat model:** ordinary roles cannot query the private feedback records or mapping; report output contains no system-added reviewer links, response IDs or response times. Trusted service/platform operators remain able to join identity and payload. This is an access-control foundation, not a production-ready anonymity promise, and does not retroactively anonymise any legacy identified records. Free-text inference, shared links, external knowledge and collusion remain outside the guarantee.

**D–E. Schema/access:** four private tables (`feedback_360_contracts`, `feedback_360_responses`, `feedback_360_identity`, `feedback_360_answers`) have RLS enabled with no ordinary-role policies and explicit revoked client grants. Service role is trusted and has access. Public `feedback_360_report(uuid)` is executable only by authenticated users and independently checks organisation-admin authority. Cross-tenant and ordinary member accounts are refused. Contract creation requires a frozen, non-empty rating/text questionnaire; its subject and minimum-response policy cannot be updated. The current foundation intentionally rejects other question types rather than converting them.

**F–G. Identity/storage:** public assignments remain the identity/delivery/completion plane. Their private one-to-one mapping points to a random feedback record; the feedback record and answers contain no person, relationship, assignment or timestamp fields. Mapping validation rejects self reviewers and mismatched subject/campaign/tenant. Submitted responses require an invitation and complete required answers. Campaign/organisation deletion cascades through private records; deleting a reviewer assignment deletes its mapping but retains the anonymous payload until campaign deletion. The subject FK prevents deleting the subject while the privacy contract exists. These retention semantics need explicit product handling in the complete workflow.

**H–I. Cohorts/comments:** fixed floor of five distinct invited external reviewers, combined across relationships; also five valid answers per displayed question. Results are closed/archived-only. No individual rating rows or reviewer groups are returned. Written comments are returned verbatim, sorted lexically per question; no system identity labels are attached, but the author may be recognisable from content. No AI redaction or mathematical anonymity claim is made.

**J–K. Monitoring/results:** existing assignment completion tracking is retained. No new 360 monitoring screen or reminder engine is exposed. The report returns `unavailable`, `not_closed`, `insufficient_responses` or `available`; available results include total completed, eligible question average/count, and eligible comments. Suppressed question contents are absent. There is no report download, per-reviewer drill-down or anonymous activity feed. The complete workflow must enforce submission deadlines/closure using campaign locks before this endpoint becomes customer-facing; trusted fixture writes are not a respondent API.

**L. Adversarial checks:** the disposable PostgreSQL runner now executes `supabase/tests/360_privacy_foundation.sql`. Tests query private data as administrator, linked subject, manager-reviewer, peer-reviewer, ordinary member, cross-tenant account and anon. Tests simulate accidental schema access and even SELECT grants: RLS still returns no private rows. They verify immutable privacy, submitted-answer protection, duplicate-reviewer rejection across relationships, required-answer/rating/cross-campaign validation, one/two/three/four-response suppression, five-response release, singleton optional-question suppression, lexical text ordering, no report linkage/chronology, unsupported public creation/activation, no activation token/outbox leakage, and campaign deletion cascades. A public identified response cannot be created for the private 360 fixture. These tests use real PostgreSQL with local Supabase auth emulation; hosted PostgREST remains a staging check.
