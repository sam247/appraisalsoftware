A reviewer list determines which parts of someone's work a 360 exercise can describe. Job titles alone do not tell you whether a person has observed the behaviours in the questionnaire. Start with the purpose of the review, then choose people with relevant experience.

This checklist is for the organiser. For a general explanation of the method, start with the [360 degree feedback guide](/360-degree-feedback).

## Define what reviewers need to have seen

Write down the two or three areas the subject wants to understand. For example, cross-team handovers, clarity when delegating, or how effectively a manager removes obstacles. Match each area to a situation where someone could observe it.

| Area | Useful observation | Weak basis for selection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Handover clarity | Worked with the person on a recent handover | Works in the same office |
| Delegation | Received work and discussed the expected outcome | Knows the manager socially |
| Collaboration | Shared a deliverable with dependencies | Attended one large presentation |

Do not ask people to score something they have not seen. The [question bank](/360-feedback-questions) can help you make prompts specific enough to answer.

## Review each proposed name

For every candidate, ask:

1. What work have they actually observed?
2. Is that experience recent enough for the agreed review period?
3. Can they describe an example rather than a reputation?
4. What perspective would be missing if they were excluded?
5. Are they being included because they are relevant, or because their answer is predictable?

The subject can suggest names, but the organiser should check the reasoning and explain how the final list is agreed. Do not quietly remove someone because they might offer difficult feedback.

## A worked cohort example

The following example is fictional. Jordan leads a project involving operations and customer support. The purpose is to understand how clearly Jordan communicates priorities when work changes.

A sensible starting list includes people who received changed priorities, a colleague who coordinated the shared deadline, and someone responsible for resolving dependencies. A senior leader who only saw the final presentation may have little to say about the day-to-day behaviour.

The organiser checks whether any working perspective is missing and whether candidates can answer the selected prompts. This creates a reasoned cohort rather than a list of endorsements. It does not make the responses an objective measure of Jordan's performance.

## Check the product's release rules separately

Appraisal Software's [anonymous 360 workflow](/360-feedback-software) needs at least five available reviewers to send. Results are combined and released after campaign closure with at least five completed responses. Each displayed question also needs five valid answers. An invitation list is therefore not a promise that a report will become available.

Do not add unsuitable reviewers merely to reach a threshold. If the relevant group is too small, discuss a different feedback conversation or postpone the exercise.

## Explain privacy before sending

Tell reviewers what the exercise is for, who can access the combined results and how written comments will be shared. The software does not put reviewer names or response times into the combined report, but a distinctive incident or writing style may still identify an author. Trusted platform operators can access operational records.

Keep personal invitation links private. Do not ask reviewers to reveal whether a particular comment was theirs after the report is shared.

## Final organiser checklist

- Purpose and review period are written down.
- Every reviewer has relevant observation.
- The cohort is not selected for favourable answers.
- Questions fit what the cohort can observe.
- Privacy and release rules have been explained.
- A person is responsible for the [results conversation](/blog/discussing-360-feedback-results).

The checklist supports organiser judgement; the product handles invitations, collection and its reporting rules. Those are different responsibilities.
