Start software selection with the review you need to run. Employee and manager appraisals collect two identified perspectives. Anonymous 360 feedback collects observations from a wider reviewer cohort under different privacy and reporting rules. Your organisation may need either or both.

This is a requirements checklist, not an independent ranking of suppliers. Appraisal Software publishes it, and our product is one possible fit.

## Describe the work before comparing products

Write down who organises the process, who responds, how many reviews overlap and who needs access to results. Separate features you need now from possibilities for a later year.

| Requirement | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Employee/manager appraisals | Separate preparation and a useful way to compare responses |
| Anonymous 360 | Identity boundaries, response thresholds, suppressed questions and comment limitations |
| Invitations and reminders | Personal-link behaviour, scheduling and supported reminder rules |
| Capacity | Employee, active-campaign and admin definitions |
| Results | What is available, to whom, and when |
| Evaluation | Trial start, end, included capacity and data after expiry |
| Adoption | What organisers, managers and participants actually need to learn |

Do not accept “anonymous” as a complete specification. Ask whether ordinary administrators can access raw reviewer-to-answer links, whether response times are exposed, and what happens when too few people answer.

## Choose a focused tool or a broader system deliberately

If your immediate need is appraisal collection and 360 feedback, a focused tool may reduce the setup you need to manage. If you also require payroll, absence, succession planning, organisation-wide goals or extensive HR integrations, assess a broader platform against those requirements.

For example, [Cogendo's performance review page](https://www.cogendo.com/online-performance-reviews/) describes its own review offering. Use provider documentation to confirm current capabilities and then test the behaviours important to you. A vendor feature page is not evidence of our comparative testing or of a guaranteed outcome.

Do not assume that this also means payroll, goals/OKR tracking, psychometric assessment or advanced analytics.

## Make the trial a small evaluation project

Choose a permitted test dataset and a willing pilot group. Do not start by uploading your entire historical appraisal archive. Define success before the trial starts:

1. The organiser can prepare the required questions and participants.
2. Invitations reach the intended test group.
3. A participant can understand, save and submit a response.
4. The organiser can tell what remains incomplete.
5. Results match the promised access and release rules.
6. The team understands paid continuation and trial expiry.

For 360, test a legitimate cohort and the published response threshold rather than expecting a report from a single test response. Avoid creating fictitious participant submissions to imply a real assessment.

## Compare total fit, not a headline price

Check whether billing is per employee or workspace, whether VAT is additional, and whether administrators or simultaneous campaigns affect the allowance. Ask about the process for increasing capacity before starting a large exercise.

Our [pricing page](/pricing) is the current source for Appraisal Software's offering. Evaluate the published trial and activation terms there; this article is not a promise of permanent free access or a downloadable app.

## Keep an honest decision record

Record the requirement, evidence observed, unresolved question and decision owner. A product that performs well in a demonstration may still need a migration or training plan. Confirm those arrangements before adoption.

For guidance on the underlying review process, see [Acas reviews and appraisals](https://www.acas.org.uk/performance-management) and [CIPD performance reviews](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/appraisals-factsheet/). These references do not endorse a particular supplier.
