A 360 report gives you material for a conversation. It does not conduct that conversation or decide what someone should do next. The organiser and subject need time to understand the feedback, check the context and agree a manageable response.

This agenda is for a development discussion. Read the [360 guide](/360-degree-feedback) first if you are choosing the purpose of the exercise.

## Prepare without investigating authors

Before the meeting, check that the results are ready to share and remind yourself what reviewers were asked. Give the subject time to read the material privately if that fits your agreed process.

Make a short note of recurring themes, specific examples and areas that need clarification. Do not turn that preparation into guessing which reviewer wrote each comment. Different people may have observed different situations; identifying a writer is not necessary to explore the behaviour.

In Appraisal Software, the [combined 360 report](/360-feedback-software#privacy) is released after closure with at least five completed responses. Questions without five valid answers are suppressed. A missing question is not a zero score or proof that a topic is unimportant.

## Use a simple meeting agenda

| Stage | Prompt | Useful outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reconnect with purpose | What did you want to understand? | Shared context |
| First reaction | What stands out, including strengths? | Space to process |
| Explore a theme | What situations might explain this? | Specific behaviour and context |
| Choose a response | What could you try differently? | One or two actions |
| Agree support | What help and check-in do you need? | Owner and review date |

Pause if the subject needs time. The meeting does not have to resolve every comment.

## Separate evidence from interpretation

A useful comment describes an action and its effect. A broad label such as “unapproachable” needs careful interpretation. Ask what observable behaviour might sit behind it, without treating a guess as an established fact.

For ratings, use the questionnaire's scale and ask what opportunities the cohort had to observe the work. An average is not a verdict about a person's character. Do not invent relationship-group scores or trends that the report does not provide.

The [feedback examples resource](/360-feedback-examples) illustrates the difference between a specific observation and a general judgement.

## A fictional discussion example

Jordan's feedback includes several observations that priorities become unclear when project deadlines change. Jordan initially sees this as criticism of how hard the team is working.

The facilitator asks: “Which part of the handover could make the next priority easier to see?” Jordan recalls sending an update that explained the delay but did not say which tasks should stop.

They agree to test a short change note: what changed, what now takes priority, and what can wait. The manager offers to review the first two notes. Four weeks later, they will check whether recipients can describe the current priority more clearly.

This is an original fictional example, not a customer result or a measured product outcome. The agreement belongs to the review conversation; the software does not automatically create or track that development action.

## Finish with a small written agreement

Record the behaviour to practise, the situation in which to use it, the support available and the date for a progress conversation. Include one strength to continue. Avoid an action list so large that nobody can tell whether anything changed.
