A useful review leaves people knowing what to continue, what to change and what support is available. The next conversation should check those agreements, rather than repeat the entire appraisal.

This structure works after an identified annual appraisal or an anonymous 360 debrief. Keep the purpose and privacy of the original exercise clear.

## Write a small action record

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Action | The behaviour or result to work towards |
| Situation | Where the action will be used |
| Owner | Who is responsible |
| Support | Time, coaching, resources or an agreed dependency |
| Evidence | What would show progress |
| Check-in | When the next conversation happens |

Use the [objectives resource](/appraisal-objectives) for work outcomes and the [development plan template](/personal-development-plan-template) for learning and practice. Do not merge every ambition into one oversized objective.

## Keep the first step achievable

“Improve communication” is difficult to act on. “Include a named owner and due date in the next three project handovers” gives the person something to practise and the manager something to discuss.

The action still needs context. If a dependency changes, discuss the adjustment rather than assessing someone against an expectation that nobody can now meet.

## A fictional follow-up example

Following a 360 conversation, Jordan agrees to make changing project priorities clearer. The first step is a short update containing what changed, what is now urgent and what can wait. Jordan's manager offers to review two updates before they are sent.

At the check-in, they discuss which recipients understood the priority and where confusion remained. They keep the useful part of the format and change an ambiguous heading. They do not claim that two updates establish a permanent performance improvement.

This is a fictional development example. Appraisal Software collects the underlying feedback; it does not automatically create, monitor or score this action plan.

## Ask four questions at the check-in

1. What did you try?
2. What happened in the situations where you used it?
3. What support helped or was missing?
4. What should continue, change or stop before the next conversation?

Look for examples and constraints. A progress check should not become a fresh surprise appraisal or an exercise in collecting more scores.

## Preserve the boundaries of the original feedback

For annual reviews, revisit the agreed employee/manager record as appropriate. For 360 feedback, discuss the combined themes without circulating guesses about individual reviewers.

Keep written records in the place and access arrangement agreed by your organisation. Use relevant existing evidence rather than creating a new survey every time someone practises a skill.

## Choose the next review deliberately

An annual appraisal may suit a wider review of work and expectations. A later 360 exercise may help explore changes visible to a wider cohort. Neither should be scheduled solely to satisfy the action record. Decide what question the new exercise would answer.
