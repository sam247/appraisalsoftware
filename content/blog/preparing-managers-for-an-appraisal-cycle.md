Managers need more than a closing date and a link to a form. They need to understand the purpose of the review, what evidence to prepare and what a useful conversation should produce. A short briefing can make those expectations explicit.

This article is for the organiser briefing managers. The [annual appraisal guide](/annual-appraisal-guide) covers the overall cycle; [appraisal comments](/appraisal-comments) covers writing individual observations.

## Use a briefing that produces decisions

| Agenda item | Decision to make |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | What the review should help employee and manager understand |
| Period | Which work and agreed expectations are being reviewed |
| Preparation | What each person should bring |
| Conversation | How differences and difficult evidence will be explored |
| Follow-up | Who records actions and checks progress |
| Software | Where each person prepares and reads responses |

Include enough time for questions. If managers interpret the same prompt differently, clarify it before the campaign starts.

## Ask for evidence across the period

A practical preparation sheet can use four columns: expectation, observed work, impact, and question for the employee. This separates observation from interpretation.

For example, “the April handover had no named next owner” is an observation. “Does not care about teamwork” is a judgement that the observation does not establish. Managers should be ready to hear context and other evidence.

Do not invent ratings rules or force a distribution because a form contains a scale. Explain any scale your organisation uses and what evidence supports each judgement.

## Practise one difficult conversation

Use a fictional scenario in the briefing. An employee rates an objective as fully achieved; the manager believes a required handover remained unfinished. Ask managers to draft a neutral opener and two questions that would help them understand the difference.

A useful opener might be: “We recorded different views of the handover. Can we compare what each of us understood the finished outcome to include?”

This is a discussion exercise, not a script to read mechanically. The existing article on [different self and manager ratings](/blog/when-self-and-manager-ratings-differ) provides further preparation.

## Explain annual and 360 roles separately

In [annual appraisal software](/employee-appraisal-software), identified employee and manager responses support the review conversation. In [anonymous 360 software](/360-appraisals), a wider reviewer cohort provides combined feedback under its privacy and release rules.

Managers must not try to identify who wrote a 360 comment or treat a suppressed question as a failed score. Tell them which material they will actually receive and how it should be discussed. Do not imply that the product provides reviewer-group comparisons or assessments it does not implement.

## End with clear ownership

Ask each manager to confirm their assignments, preparation date and meeting plan. Name the organiser who can resolve setup questions. Agree where follow-up actions will be recorded; software collection is not a substitute for that agreement.

Provide links to the [questions resource](/appraisal-questions), [manager examples](/appraisal-comments) and [follow-up checklist](/blog/following-up-after-a-performance-review), rather than sending another long document that repeats the briefing.
